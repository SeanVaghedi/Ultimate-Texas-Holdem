Learning to Play Ultimate Texas Hold'em: Tabular Reinforcement Learning Approach
Problem & "Why RL?": Ultimate Texas Hold'em (UTH) is a casino poker variant where a player makes a sequence of betting decisions against a dealer across three stages: pre-flop, flop, and river, with each decision depending on partial information about progressively revealed cards. This is fundamentally a sequential decision-making problem under uncertainty: the value of an early action (e.g., raising 4x pre-flop) cannot be evaluated in isolation, since it commits the player to a trajectory whose payoff depends on future random reveals. A supervised model could classify hands as strong or weak, but cannot perform credit assignment across stages. RL is the natural fit, in which the agent must learn a policy mapping states to actions that maximize cumulative reward.
MDP Formulation: State Space (finite, discrete via abstraction). Raw card states are intractable, so we bucket by hand strength at each stage. Pre-flop: ~15 hole-card categories (high pair, suited ace, offsuit junk, etc.). Flop: ~8 categories combining made-hand strength and draw potential. River: ~7 final-hand categories. Total ≈ 840 states, which is comfortably tabular.
Action Space (finite, stage-dependent). Pre-flop: {check, bet 3x, bet 4x}. Flop (if checked pre-flop): {check, bet 2x}. River (if checked through): {fold, bet 1x}. Each stage uses its own Q-table.
Reward Function. Zero at intermediate decisions, at showdown, the net chip outcome under authentic UTH rules:  Ante (paid only if dealer qualifies), Play bet (even money), and the Blind bonus table (escalating payouts for straights, flushes, etc.). Faithful payouts are critical, in which a simplified ±1 reward would erase the incentive to raise aggressively with strong hands, which the Blind bonus exists to reward.
Environment and Data Strategy: We will build a custom Python simulator (~150 lines) handling dealing, dealer qualification, and UTH payouts, using the "treys"  library for hand evaluation. Training generates experience online over 1–5 million simulated hands.
Baselines and Evaluation: Primary methods: Monte Carlo control with ε-greedy exploration (well-suited to short episodic hands with terminal rewards), compared against tabular Q-learning.
Baselines: (1) random play, (2) always-fold, (3) a naive heuristic ("raise 4x with any pair or ace"), and (4) the published optimal UTH strategy as a performance ceiling (≈ -2.2% house edge).
Evaluation: Freeze the policy (ε = 0) and measure expected value per hand over 500,000 evaluation hands. We report convergence curves, compare MC vs. Q-learning, and visualize the learned policy as a strategy chart against the published optimal, yielding insight into where tabular RL struggles on rarely visited states.
